HAC-D User Manual

Availability, MTBF, MTTR and goodput modeling for AI training and inference clusters: closed forms with validity flags, Monte Carlo where the closed forms do not hold, every number with its formula and its 口径 (event, denominator, level). Version 3.1, 2026-10-11.

1. What HAC-D is

HAC-D turns a cluster description (topology, component classes with failure and repair parameters, workload, recovery policy, fault-management mechanisms) into availability, mean time between interruptions, recovery time, goodput and cost figures, and then helps allocate a target back to subsystems and mechanisms. It is grounded in the survey chapter AI 集群可靠性建模 and cross-checked against an independent reference engine (an audited 30-day discrete-event simulator of one training job).

1.1 Principles

  • Every number carries its formula and its 口径. A rate is an event class per denominator at a level (for example hard interruptions per card-hour at job level). Quantities of different event classes are never added.
  • Closed forms first, with validity flags. The first-order goodput checks τ + δ ≪ M and R ≪ M; when the flags fire the headline switches to the exact renewal form.
  • Monte Carlo when the assumptions break. Non-exponential lifetimes, correlated failures, finite spare pools, finite windows and silent corruption route to simulation; the closed-form result stays visible with a delta badge.
  • Provenance on every parameter. Library records and scenario inputs carry source, year and confidence (measured, paper-reported, vendor, derived, assumed, scan-level).

3.2 Fault classes

Three manifestations are modelled per component class, each with its own 口径 and its own handling:

  • Interruption (中断): the unit stops; the job is detected down, recovers through the mechanism chain and the global checkpoint recovery, rolls back to the last checkpoint unless a no-rollback rung handled it.
  • Sub-health (亚健康, fail-slow): the unit keeps running at a performance factor φ for a duration; the synchronous job is slowed (straggler law); the policy is tolerate or isolate (detected units are switched out after saving progress).
  • Silent corruption (静默错误, SDC): the state is corrupted without an interruption; immediate detection catches a share, undetected corruption is discovered after a fallback delay and rolled back to the last clean checkpoint; still-dirty state at the end of the window is discarded.

3.3 Confidence and provenance

Each rate or time may carry provenance (source, organisation, year, URL, derivation) and a confidence tag. The library (§12.2) uses the same tags; the reference examples tag borrowed shares and targets as assumed. The planner (§6.3) derates assumed mechanism parameters; the Allocation page weights feasibility by confidence.

4. Scenario page

4.1 Topology and supernode presets

GPUs per node, nodes per rack, racks per zone, scale-up domain size and network planes define the count scopes (per gpu, node, rack, zone, fixed). A Supernode preset (CloudMatrix384 or a parametrised Atlas 950) adds the supernode geometry: NPUs per node, compute and communication racks, UB sub-planes, L2 chips per sub-plane and ports, L1 chips and links per node, transceivers per link. Component classes can then count per supernode, communication rack, L1 or L2 switch, sub-plane, optical link or transceiver. For CloudMatrix384 the derived counts are 48 nodes × 8 NPU, 7 sub-planes × 16 L2 chips, 5,376 optical links; fields the public sources do not give are marked assumed.

4.3 Workload and recovery

Training: job GPUs / NPUs (or supernodes used), duration, exposed checkpoint save δ, interval model (Young, Daly three-term, Daly exact, Saxena) or a fixed interval, hot-spare nodes and replenishment, recovery path (checkpoint τ/2 or replica one step), measured lost computation W. Inference: replicas required, GPUs per replica (one supernode as the unit), spare replicas, capacity target, SLO window.

Recovery: a pipeline of stages with scale laws (presets baseline-timeout-manual, automated-probe-standby, trainmover-8192-restart, elastic-replica), a ladder of rungs with success probabilities (mindcluster-a3, generic-auto-restart, or custom), or the MTTR mixture R = T_det + T_auto + (1 − p_s) T_manual with T_det = p_d t_auto + (1 − p_d) t_timeout.

4.4 Silent-corruption policy and mechanism chain

Job-level: immediate detection coverage q and time, fallback discovery delay D (120 h in the reference baseline), optional step recovery (redo at most t_step, recover in t_rec). The recovery mechanism chain enables replay (no rollback), DP-domain elasticity (no rollback; one of n domains out for t_el, global sync t_sync) and process rescheduling (rollback), each with success probability and success / failure times; they are tried in that order before the global checkpoint recovery.

4.5 Measures, targets, costs, JSON

Sub-health threshold φ_min (default 0.7: below it sub-health counts as down), communication share for fabric-plane loss, headline measure (compensated / original), goodput and availability targets, $ per GPU-hour. The scenario JSON editor at the bottom shows the full document (schema in docs/scenario.schema.json).

5. Training page

5.1 Reading the page

  • Delivered goodput is the hero tile when the scenario has silent corruption; the effective goodput (performance) and the delivery factor are shown beside it.
  • Regime line: (τ + δ)/M and R/M with the validity badge; the headline model (first order while ok, otherwise exact renewal).
  • Models table: A_op, first order ★, product form, exact renewal (with the implied rollback W), Daly full model, with the assumption of each.
  • Window ledger: delivered + downtime + saves + rollback + slow-down + elastic domain loss + spares + SDC rollback + terminal discard = 1 (closed form, decomposed with the headline model).
  • Mechanism chain per class: applicable fractions, P(replay / elastic / process / global), P(rollback), mean pause, domain loss per event.
  • Scale extrapolation: 1/N law with the chain, pause-only events and the SDC window applied per row; caution / invalid badges where the first-order flags fire; a supernodes column under a supernode preset.

6.2 Attribution

Select the players and the value function (delivered goodput, effective goodput or A_op), press Attribute: the selected mechanisms are stripped from the scenario (baseline), all 2ⁿ coalitions are evaluated with the closed forms, and the report gives single gains (each alone), last-added gains (added to all the others) and exact Shapley values, which sum to the combined gain. Substitutes (replay, elasticity, process rescheduling) show diminishing returns; complements (detection and step recovery) show up only together.

6.3 Plan to target

Set the target, press Plan: every coalition is evaluated nominally and with the library's assumed parameters derated (coverage −5 points, success −8 points, times ×1.5); a coalition is feasible when both reach the target. The card reports the cheapest feasible set by effort, the mechanisms present in every feasible set (necessary), the effort–value Pareto front, a scan of silent-corruption detection coverage × automatic recovery time on top of the plan, and, when enabled, a window-ledger simulation of the plan with P(window ≥ target) against a confidence level. Effort scores and target parameters are editable in the library table.

Reference three-class baseline, target 90 % delivered: at 50,000 cards D-SDC alone is cheapest and necessary (95.4 % nominal, 94.2 % derated, 95 % of months ≥ 90 %); at 100,000 cards it passes on the mean (92.3 %) but only 85 % of months reach 90 %, so add replay or elasticity for margin; at 500,000 cards no library set suffices (83.4 %) and the scan shows coverage ≥ 99 % with recovery ≤ 120 s is needed, the reference tool's own conclusion.

7.2 Goodput budget and target basis

With the Young interval the first-order loss is L = √(2δ/M) + R/M. Given a target G*, the page solves the required MTBI (keep R), the required recovery time (keep λ), and a balanced split, then apportions the required interruption rate to the classes and the required recovery time to the stages. In the slack case the tables show how far each class may degrade (improvement factors below 1).

On the delivered basis (default when silent corruption is present) a delivered target G_d is converted into effective and interruption targets (÷ delivery factor ÷ sub-health factor); the page first checks whether the hardware alone can reach it (rates → 0 under the current silent-corruption policy) and, if not, reports the smallest immediate detection coverage q* and the longest fallback delay D* that do, and computes the budgets with the coverage raised to q*.

8. Inference, spares and domains

Inference: an N+k replica pool with one replica = a node, rack or supernode; replica rate λ_r = Σ classes at or below the replica scope, MTTR_r rate-weighted, u_r = λ_r MTTR_r / (1 + λ_r MTTR_r), service-up availability 1 − u_r^{n+k}, capacity availability Σ_{j≤k} C(n+k, j) u_r^j (1 − u_r)^{n+k−j}, the k required for the target, replica failures per day and idle spare GPU-hours. Under a supernode preset the page sizes 100k / 200k / 500k-NPU clusters in supernodes (for CloudMatrix384 analogue rates: 261 / 521 / 1,303 supernodes, k* = 9 / 14 / 27 spares for 99.9 % capacity availability).

Spares & domains: hot spares by binomial quantile (ByteRobust), by Poisson with replenishment time (Meta), units in the repair pipeline and replacements over the job, a required-spares map over node MTBF × MTTR, and spare granularity matched to the failure domain.

9. Compare page

The tornado halves and doubles each component rate, the recovery time, the checkpoint δ, the hot spares and the common-cause β, for goodput, delivered goodput, availability, lost $/day or MTBI. Use it for parameter sensitivity; use the Mechanisms page for the value of discrete mechanisms.

10. Simulate page

Window ledger (default): a discrete-event simulation of one job window with the three fault classes, the mechanism chain (Bernoulli applicability), sub-health detection and isolation, silent corruption (immediate detection, step recovery, fallback discovery, clean-checkpoint retention, terminal discard) and checkpoint batching; the same semantics as the audited reference engine, every hour booked once. Job simulation: unit-level Monte Carlo with Weibull / lognormal lifetimes and repairs, infant mortality, lemon units, β-factor common cause with blast radius, finite hot-spare pool, redundancy groups and fail-slow. Both compare with the closed form and flag disagreement; prefer the simulation when it disagrees.

13. Methods reference

Full derivations: docs/MODEL_REFERENCE.md (§1–§22.2) and docs/TRAINING_AVAILABILITY_FORMULAS_AND_COMPARISON.md. Times in seconds unless noted.

13.1 Structure

series: A = Π Aᵢ, λ = Σ λᵢ · parallel: U = Π Uᵢ · k-of-n (repairable): Σ_{j≤n−k} C(n,j) uʲ (1−u)ⁿ⁻ʲ, u = λ·MTTR/(1 + λ·MTTR) standby: CTMC with standby factor and switch probability · common cause: β-factor on the declared domain · cut sets and importance from the Boolean structure

13.2 Repair paths and switchover

E[R] = c·T_a + (1 − c·s)·T_m; A = 1/(1 + λ·E[R]); human fraction = 1 − c·s; automatic share of downtime = c·T_a/E[R] k-of-n group: exact chain over (in automatic repair, in manual repair, switch-failed); micro-outage U_sw = Σ π·w·λ·p_sw·t_sw/3600; failed switchover cleared in T_sf

13.3 Sub-health

unit: states U, H, H′, I, D_a, D_m; A_strict = π_U; A_tolerant = π_U + π_H; performability = π_U + φ·π_H; down when φ < threshold (0.7) job: P(slowed) = 1 − Π (1 − p_H,i)^{nᵢ}; φ_job = 1 − P(slowed)(1 − φ_min); isolate: ν_iso = π_H·p_d/T_d pause-only events (progress saved first) fabric plane loss: performance 1/(1 + f_comm/(P − 1))

13.4 Original vs compensated

original: every unit fault is an outage repaired manually, redundancy removed: λ_o = λ + λ_sh, E[R_o] = (λ T_m + λ_sh D)/(λ + λ_sh) waterfall: original → + redundancy → + automatic recovery → + sub-health policy; switchover penalty reported separately

13.5 Checkpointing and goodput

interval: Young τ = √(2δM_rb); Daly three-term and exact; Saxena availability-optimal period MTTR mixture: R = T_det + T_auto + (1 − p_s)·T_manual, T_det = p_d·t_auto + (1 − p_d)·t_timeout first order: G ≈ 1 − δ/τ − (P_rb·W + R̄)/M_ev, W = τ/2 (or one step / measured); flags (τ+δ)/M and R/M exact renewal (mixed): G = λ_rb τ / [(e^{λ_rb(τ+δ)} − 1)(1 + λ_rb R_rb + λ_p R_p)]; implied W = E[min(X, τ) | X < τ+δ] product: A_run · τ/(τ+δ) · (1 − P_rb W/M_ev); Daly full: τ/(M e^{R/M}(e^{(τ+δ)/M} − 1)) · 1/(1 + λ_p R_p) headline: first order while the flags are ok, otherwise exact renewal; effective = headline × (1 − P_blocked) × φ_job × (1 − ℓ_local)

ν = λ_sdc (1 − q) · A_run · (1 − P_clear), P_clear = 1 − (1 − e^{−λ_rb L})/(λ_rb L) (a rollback before the first dirty checkpoint clears the corruption) S_k = Γ(k, ν) + (k − 1)(D + R_c); P(S_k ≤ y) = P(Poisson(νz) ≥ k), z = y − (k − 1)c E[N] = Σ_k P(S_k ≤ T); E[dirty] = Σ_k ∫₀ᴰ P(S_k ≤ T − x) dx; E[pause] = Σ_k ∫_D^c P(S_k ≤ T − x) dx delivered = [g·(T − E[dirty] − E[pause]) − U·E[N]]/T, U = (τ²/2 + τδ)/(τ + δ); detected corruption: rollback event with pause t_d + G, or pause-only with step recovery

13.8 Attribution and planning

Shapley: φᵢ = Σ_{S∌i} |S|!(n−|S|−1)!/n! · [v(S ∪ {i}) − v(S)], Σ φᵢ = v(all) − v(∅); interaction = Δ − Σ single gains planner: feasible S ⇔ v(S) ≥ G* and v_derated(S) ≥ G*; plan = argmin effort; necessary = ∩ feasible S; Pareto over (effort, v); verification P(window ≥ G*) ≥ confidence

13.9 Allocation and spares

goodput budget (Young): L = √(2δ/M) + R/M; fix R: √(2δ) s + R s² = L*, s = M^{−1/2}; fix M: R* = M (L* − √(2δ/M)) delivered basis: effective target = G_d / f_SDC; headline target = effective / (φ_job (1 − P_blocked)(1 − ℓ)); q*, D* by bisection availability split: ln Aᵢ = (wᵢ/Σw) ln A*; λᵢ = Uᵢ/(MTTRᵢ(1 − Uᵢ)) hot spares: smallest k with P(Binomial(N_nodes, p_daily) ≤ k) ≥ q; Poisson(N λ T_replenish) quantile; repair pipeline Poisson(N λ f_hw L) quantile inference: u_r = λ_r MTTR_r/(1 + λ_r MTTR_r); A_any = 1 − u_r^{n+k}; A_cap = Σ_{j≤k} C(n+k, j) u_r^j (1 − u_r)^{n+k−j}

15. Recommended workflow

  1. Fix the 口径 and gather data: which metric (delivered goodput, effective goodput, A_op, capacity availability), which window; rates from your own logs where possible, library records with provenance otherwise.
  2. Build and evaluate the scenario: topology and classes, repair paths, sub-health and silent corruption, workload and recovery; read the Training (or Inference) page and the regime flags.
  3. Attribute: where does the loss go (ledger), which levers matter (Shapley, tornado).
  4. Allocate and plan: on the Allocation page apportion the interruption budget; on the Mechanisms page pick the cheapest sufficient mechanism set with derating.
  5. Verify: window-ledger simulation with P(window ≥ target); job simulation when lifetimes or correlations are non-exponential.
  6. Review and report: the eleven-item checklist, original vs compensated measures, exports; then measure in production and loop.

17. Limitations and roadmap

  • Closed forms assume exponential lifetimes and repairs and independent failures (common cause only through β); the simulations relax this.
  • The mechanism chain's elastic rung is a capacity model (one domain out, the others continue); it is not a synchronous-training performance promise and needs the prerequisites listed in the library.
  • Silent corruption uses a fixed fallback delay and a single episode at a time, as the reference does; retention windows and locate / verify success rates are not separate parameters yet.
  • Mechanism parameters of the library are targets and priors from the reference catalog (tagged); derating is a convention, not a measurement.
  • Open items: the 54-event catalog converter, a DP-elastic training template, Monte Carlo confidence intervals for Shapley values, image / diagram import.

silent data corruption: corrupted state without an interruption
正确有效产出
delivered goodput: correct progress retained at the end of the window ÷ window length
MTBI / MTBF / MTTR
mean time between interruptions (job) / between failures (system) / to repair
A_op
operational availability: running-time share of the job
τ, δ, W, R
checkpoint interval, exposed save time, lost computation per rollback, recovery time
Shapley value
fair share of a combined gain attributed to one mechanism over all orders of adding the mechanisms
supernode (超节点)
a scale-up domain such as CloudMatrix384 (384 NPUs, 48 nodes, 12 compute + 4 communication racks)
