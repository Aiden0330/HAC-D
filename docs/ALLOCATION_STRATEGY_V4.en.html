How v4 splits a target between hardware failure rates and reliability mechanisms — method, parameters used, a 500,000-NPU example and the three versions compared

Basis：A_op = running-time share over the 30-day job window, target the scenario default 98.5 %; it must hold with nominal and with derated parameters (coverage −5 pt; success, recall and precision −8 pt; times and overheads ×1.5; rate reductions of physical mechanisms ×0.5 — applied to the scenario's own mechanisms, the candidates and the levers). FIT = business-impact events per 10⁹ unit-hours after the existing protection, converted from the 54-event reference catalog to hardware units under the CloudMatrix384 geometry (assumed values). Built-in scales: 10k / 100k / 200k / 500k NPUs. Code: packages/core/src/strategy/index.ts; every number here is computed on the engine by scripts/allocation-strategy-v4-example.ts.

2. The three strategies and the hardware split

StrategyRuleUse when
Optimalleast total effort (mechanisms + levers + hardware) over all subsets of the candidate moves (A_op: exact enumeration up to 10 moves, the hardware level in closed form, the chosen plan confirmed on the engine; delivered goodput: exact up to 6, greedy descent on the total effort beyond); the cheapest plans are listed as alternativesthe effort scores are trusted and the cheapest combination is wanted
MTTR reduction preferredrecovery side first: among the non-physical moves only (detection, recovery and checkpoint mechanisms, the three levers), add at each step the one with the largest gain of the binding value (the lower of nominal and derated) per effort until the target holds or nothing gains; the remaining gap goes to hardwarehardware changes are slow or expensive; software and operations go first
MTTF reduction preferredhardware first: close the gap with hardware alone; if the floors are not enough, add physical mechanisms (e.g. H-LEMON, H-SWEEP; none among the default candidates) and then recovery-side moves with the hardware held at its floors, then relax the hardware back to just meeting the targetthe hardware design is still open (selection, supplier requirements)
Hardware split (level rule): for a level L, xᵢ·cᵢ = clamp(wᵢ·L, floorᵢ·cᵢ, cᵢ), where cᵢ = λᵢ·p̄ᵢ is the pause per hour caused by class i (rate × mean pause per event of its mechanism chain) and L is found by bisection on the engine so that the target just holds. The weight wᵢ sets the method: minimum effort (default) wᵢ = eᵢ minimises Σ eᵢ log₂(1/xᵢ) for the A_op budget (KKT: improved classes end at xᵢcᵢ ∝ eᵢ; with equal e it is Albert’s minimum effort on pause contributions); AGREE wᵢ = units × importance (complexity weight, slack of the classes under budget reallocated); ARINC wᵢ = cᵢ (one factor for every class). For A_op the running fraction is exactly 1/(1 + (Σ xᵢcᵢ + F)/3600), F = sub-health isolation, predictor false alarms and detected silent corruption, so the level has a closed form. When the target already holds the result reports the hardware slack: the uniform factor by which every interruption FIT could grow with the target kept.

The strategies do not allocate mechanism parameter values. Mechanisms enter with the library parameters (or the user’s edits on the Mechanisms page) and the three recovery parameters can only move to their limits as a whole; there is no continuous solve for “how much coverage / how fast a recovery is needed”. Visible consequences: ① the derated automatic-success lever is 99.9 % − 8 pt = 91.9 %, below today’s 99 %, so it has no derated effect and the strategies never pick it; ② MTTR-preferred adds a mechanism with an almost zero gain (D-SLOW); ③ delivered-goodput targets are capped by the checkpoint and silent-corruption parameters: here derated C-FAST saves 1.5 s every 60 s and derated D-SDC covers 90 %, so even with every hardware class at its floor the derated delivered goodput tops out at 76.63 % (nominal 86.45 %) and ≥ 90 % is reported infeasible — what is needed then is a parameter allocation (e.g. the required silent-corruption detection q*, which the Classic methods tab computes for delivered targets together with the fallback delay D*), not more hardware.

6. Is it the same as the earlier allocation methods?

Not the same, but built from the same parts. The v3.0 classic methods (equal, ARINC, AGREE, feasibility, Albert minimum effort, cost optimal) and the v3.1 mechanism-only planner are unchanged in code and remain on the Allocation page’s Classic methods and Mechanism planner tabs; the v3.7 allocation study sits below the Cross-scale tab. 8. Best practices

  1. Fix topology and scale first, then replace the data. Start from a template with the six defaults; where you have measurements, replace the library FIT by per-unit values of your cluster (Garwood interval for event counts) and keep source and confidence.
  2. Choose the 口径 and per-size targets first. Do not mix A_op (running time) with delivered goodput (retained progress); one target for all sizes can become infeasible at large N because the F term (isolation, false alarms, detected silent corruption) grows with N, so set targets per size.
  3. Take v4.0 Optimal as the reference answer and price preferences with the other two. The effort gap between Optimal and MTTR- / MTTF-preferred is the price of the preference.
  4. Require nominal and fully derated feasibility, and verify the final plan with the ledger. Read P(window ≥ target) with its Wilson interval; derating only the added mechanisms (v3.7) overstates the margin.
  5. Calibrate efforts and floors, and test sensitivity. Effort scores, effort per halving and floors are assumptions; for A3 · CloudMatrix384 at 200k the 60 s automatic attempt (3), hardware alone (5.19) and v3.7’s D-HARD + C-MEM (5) are close, so a ±1 change of a score flips the choice.
  6. Do not size with first-order budgets when a mechanism chain is active. v3.0’s λ* assumes every interruption pays the global recovery time; use it only for designs without recovery rungs or as a first screen.
  7. Read hardware results per unit and use them as acceptance values. Give suppliers the allowed FIT and MTBF (years) per unit; when the target already holds, use the slack factor as the acceptance ceiling.
  8. Look at the classes without a recovery rung first. Here 80 % of the pause comes from classes no rung covers (UNKNOWN, POWER, MGMT, STORAGE, COOL, FABRIC, BOXIO); a local recovery rung for them would pay more than further FIT cuts (the library has no such mechanism yet — a candidate for the next step).
  9. Delivered-goodput targets need parameter allocation. Use the classic q*, D* and stage budgets, edit D-SDC coverage, C-FAST save time and similar parameters on the Mechanisms page, then re-check with v4.0.
  10. Cover the largest size you must support, and keep the designs. Requirements grow with N (here 10k / 100k have slack, 500k needs all three kinds of moves); re-check on the Cross-scale tab, save the chosen design as a scenario and export the report.

Limitations and suggested next steps

  • Continuous allocation of mechanism parameters (required coverage, recovery time, save time) is not in v4.0 yet; delivered-goodput targets need it most.
  • The success lever should be derated on its failure probability ×1.5 (99.9 % → 99.85 %), otherwise it never helps under derating; MTTR-preferred could use a minimum-gain threshold.
  • The v3.7 study’s derated check should be aligned with v4.0 (derate the scenario’s own mechanisms too); otherwise its plans at 200k / 500k fail the common check.
