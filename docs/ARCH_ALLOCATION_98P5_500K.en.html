Allocating A_op 98.5 % at 500,000 NPUs: ARINC on the hardware, mechanisms on the recovery side, across six Ascend architectures

Subjects: the 910C’s CloudMatrix384 and Atlas 800T A3 server cluster (ASCEND910C_SCALING_RELIABILITY), the 950’s native Atlas 950 SuperPoD and UBL128 (ASCEND950_SCALING_RELIABILITY), each at its report’s main case; HAC-D’s template-default A2 (910B) and Atlas 960E as reference rows (grey). Target: A_op ≥ 98.5 % for one synchronous training job over a 30-day window, met both with nominal parameters and with every mechanism derated (coverage −5 pt, success −8 pt, times ×1.5, the six defaults included) — the 口径 of HAC-D’s allocation strategies. Hardware is allocated by ARINC: every interruption class’s FIT times one factor x; the recovery side picks from HAC-D’s default candidates (D-HARD, D-SLOW, P-PREDICT, R-NETFAIL, R-HOTSWAP, C-MEM) and three recovery levers (automatic attempt ≥ 60 s, automatic success ≤ 99.9 %, manual ≥ 30 min).

  • Gap: with the six default mechanisms, the four main architectures have, at 500k, an A_op of 95.59–96.36 % nominal and 95.10–95.91 % derated. 98.5 % allows ≈ 46–47 s of pause per hour against 128–157 s today (146–177 s derated): about 68–74 % of the pause time has to go.
  • ① Hardware alone (ARINC): every interruption class’s FIT times one factor ×0.26–0.32 (hardest for CM384, easiest for UBL128) — 879–981 FIT per NPU and a cluster MTBF_hw ≥ 2.0–2.3 h; or, at today’s FIT, MTTR_hw down to 27–31 s.
  • ② / ③ Mechanisms first, then hardware: D-HARD (99.9 % of interruptions detected within 1 s) + R-NETFAIL (95 % of network faults failed over inside the collective in 2 s) + automatic attempt 180 → 60 s + manual recovery 2 h → 30 min reach 98.71–98.87 % nominally without touching the hardware, but only 97.60–97.87 % derated; ARINC ×0.60–0.68 closes the rest: FIT only has to fall by 32–40 % (① needs 68–74 %). On UBL128 R-NETFAIL’s gain (+0.20 points alone, derated) only matches its effort score, so ③ leaves it out. Final: 99.19–99.20 % nominal, exactly 98.50 % derated, all 400 30-day windows ≥ 98.5 %.
  • Architectures: the two 950 architectures have lower FIT per NPU (3,043 / 3,097 against 3,301 / 3,384 for 910C) and a looser hardware factor (③ ×0.68 / ×0.66 against ×0.60 / ×0.60); within a generation the two forms differ by less than 0.03. After allocation the residual downtime sits in the classes without a fast recovery rung: job-level services 48–55 %, compute cabinets 13–24 %, nodes 14–20 %.
  • 口径: the target is A_op (= ReMAP’s “operational availability”), not ReMAP’s headline “availability” (delivered goodput): the final designs deliver only 85.7–85.9 %. ARINC shares the cut in proportion to today’s rates (one factor) — simple but costly: a minimum-effort split that only touches the 3–5 classes without a fast rung (UNKNOWN ×0.19–0.24, POWER, MGMT, STORAGE, COOL) needs about a third of the hardware effort (right of Table 2).

Delivered goodput (retained correct progress ÷ window) also loses checkpoint saves, rollback recompute and silent corruption, ≈ 86 % — bringing ReMAP’s headline “availability” to 98.5 % is another and much harder problem (C-FAST’s save overhead alone costs 1.6 points).

How to read it and limits

  • What ARINC means: allowed values in proportion to today’s rates, i.e. “every class improves by the same factor”. It ignores how hard each improvement is and which classes have a fast recovery rung; HAC-D also offers AGREE and the minimum-effort split (right of Table 2), which concentrates the hardware task on the global-recovery classes UNKNOWN, POWER, MGMT, STORAGE and COOL.
  • Mechanism allocation: candidates are HAC-D’s 6 allocation-page defaults (evaluable without integration parameters); the extended library (35 entries incl. the 16 audited mechanisms with provisional parameters) is not used. D-HARD and the two recovery levers are chosen for every architecture — the three best buys; R-NETFAIL does not pay on UBL128 and A2.
  • Uncertainty: failure rates are the 54-event reference catalog (an analogue prior from GPU fleets) converted per unit, not 910C / 950 measurements; mechanism parameters are nominal targets; structural assumptions (800T A3 servers per cabinet, scale-out ports and tiers) are in the two scaling reports. The shape of the result (a gap of ≈ 3×, mechanisms first halve the hardware task, residual downtime in the global-recovery classes) does not hinge on them; the exact factors move with them.
