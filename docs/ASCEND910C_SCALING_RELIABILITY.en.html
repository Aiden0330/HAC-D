Ascend 910C in two deployment forms at 10k–500k NPUs: hardware scale, reliability and bottlenecks

Subjects: the Atlas 900 A3 SuperPoD / CloudMatrix384 (one UB domain of 384 NPUs: 48 nodes, 12 compute + 4 communication cabinets, two-level UB switching) and an Atlas 800T A3 server cluster (the same 8-NPU board used stand-alone: one 10U server is an 8-NPU UB domain, RoCE between servers). Hardware counts come from the HAC-D v4.3 engine’s a3-ub384 template (800T A3 = the same template without the L2 tier, the UB optics and the communication cabinets, Section 2); reliability is that of one synchronous training job with the six default mechanisms (D-SDC, R-SDC, R-REPLAY, R-PROCESS, R-ELASTIC, C-FAST, Section 5). Both use the same scale-out: 2 × 200G per NPU, 64-port switches, non-blocking folded Clos (HAC-D template default, main case); other designs in 3.4. Companion: docs/ASCEND950_SCALING_RELIABILITY.en.html; the 98.5 % allocation at 500k: docs/ARCH_ALLOCATION_98P5_500K.en.html; Section 6 gives the Shapley contributions of the latest ReMAP mechanism set (23).

  • Hardware: compute is the same in both forms (one 8-NPU board, 32 NPUs per cabinet; 15,625 compute cabinets and 250,000 CPUs at 500k); the difference is the UB domain: per 384 NPUs the CM384 has 4 communication cabinets, 112 L2 chips and 5,376 L1–L2 optical links — 5,209 cabinets, 146k L2 chips and 7M UB optical links at 500k; one 800T A3 server is an 8-NPU UB domain and has none of these. The scale-out is the same for both: four tiers, 109,375 switches at 500k.
  • Reliability with scale: CM384 A_op 99.91 % (10k) → 99.09 % (100k) → 98.19 % (200k) → 95.59 % (500k); MTBF_hw from 29.7 h down to 0.591 h (35 min), MTTR_hw ≈ 93 s at every size. The 800T A3 lacks the UB optics and the L2 tier (83.3 FIT per NPU, ≈ 2.5 %) and is 0.002–0.10 points higher — but its UB domain is only 8 NPUs, a cost on the performance side (not in this model). The numbers equal the 910C column of the other “950 and 910C” report item by item (Section 4).
  • Thresholds: below 99 % from ≈ 109k (CM384) / 112k (800T A3) NPUs, below 98.5 % from ≈ 165k / 169k (almost exactly the stated CM384 cluster ceiling of 165,000), MTBF_hw under 1 h from ≈ 296k. At 500k, 98.5 % needs FIT per NPU down to 991 (today 3,384) or MTTR down to 27 s.
  • Bottleneck: the classes without a fast recovery rung — job-level shared services (storage, unattributed / software), management and board IO, cabinet power / cooling, UB and RoCE switches — are 32 % of the hardware interruptions but 90 % of the downtime (264.5 s of global recovery each). The NPU itself is 50 % of the interruptions but only 8 % of the downtime. Larger than the difference between the forms is how many servers share a cabinet: with 2 per cabinet the 800T A3 loses another 0.92 points at 500k.
  • 口径 and limits: failure rates and mechanism parameters are the values of the one catalog shared by ReMAP and ai-cluster-reliability (54 events, FIT per card, an analogue prior from GPU fleets; all 8 checks pass, see HACD_REMAP_COMPARISON); HAC-D converts them per hardware unit under the CloudMatrix384 geometry — so the CM384 without scale-out returns the repositories’ values (reference row of Table 1) and the only difference is the scale-out network. None are 910C measurements. Servers per cabinet and the RoCE ports of the 800T A3 are assumed; the scale-out radix and oversubscription are not public.

  • 10k NPUs: 0.81 interruptions per day, MTBF_hw 29.7 h, A_op 99.908 %, about 17× headroom for 98.5 %. The bottleneck is not availability but single long stops: every interruption of the job-level services, cabinet power / cooling and switches goes to the global recovery (264.5 s). Among 27 CM384 pods, a power loss in one communication cabinet takes ≈ 28 L2 chips out (≈ 1/4 of every sub-plane if they are spread evenly).
  • 100k NPUs: ≈ 8.1 interruptions per day, MTBF_hw 2.96 h; both forms still meet 99 % (≈ 1.10× headroom). CM384 falls below 99 % beyond ≈ 109k NPUs, the 800T A3 beyond ≈ 112k. The scale-out goes from three to four tiers at ≈ 33k NPUs (radix 64, 2 ports per NPU).
  • 200k NPUs: ≈ 16.2 interruptions per day, MTBF_hw 1.48 h; A_op 98.187 % / 98.230 %, both already below 98.5 % (FIT per NPU must fall to 2,758, ×1.23). The 98.5 % ceiling is ≈ 165k / 169k NPUs — right at the stated CM384 cluster ceiling of 165,000; 200k and 500k themselves are beyond it, an extrapolation.

3.3 Single points of failure and blast radius (Table 5)

A synchronous training job is a series system: any hard interruption stops the whole job, so every component is a single point for the job.
