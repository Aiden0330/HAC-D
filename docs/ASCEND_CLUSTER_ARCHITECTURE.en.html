Typical Ascend AI-cluster architectures and topologies: cluster · network · SuperPoD · hardware

This page draws the structure of Ascend clusters from Huawei’s public material in four pictures: the five levels (Figure 1), three generations of topology (Figure 2), the CloudMatrix384 UB fabric and its three planes (Figure 3), and where the 18 hardware domains of the 54-event reference catalog sit (Figure 4). Numbers come from the source records already in the project (docs/ASCEND_SOURCES.md, with URL and confidence) and from the search summaries listed at the end; unit counts are computed by HAC-D’s computeCounts with the cloudmatrix384 / atlas950 presets. Confidence labels: paper = stated in the paper body; vendor = Huawei release or documentation; derived = computed from those; scan-level = seen only in third-party pages or search summaries; assumed = a fill-in value of the HAC-D preset. The in-cabinet and communication-cabinet internals of Atlas 950 / 960 are not public.

UB-Mesh (Huawei paper and Hot Chips 2025 talk) describes a 2D-FullMesh of 64 NPUs per rack, a 4D-FullMesh at pod level and Clos above; its 8K example is 128 racks × 64 NPUs, the same counts as Atlas 950, but the product releases do not say the product uses that topology, so Figure 2 is not drawn from it.

  • Three nested levels, two networks: NPU → node (8 NPUs) → SuperPoD (one UB scale-up domain) → cluster (RoCE or UBoE scale-out). The scale-up domain grew from 8 (A2 server) to 384 (A3 / CloudMatrix384) and to 8,192 / 15,488 (Atlas 950 / 960), so more and more of the collective traffic stays inside UB.
  • Three planes: UB (inside the domain, 392 GB/s per NPU), RDMA / RoCE (between domains, 400 Gb/s per NPU), VPC (Qingtian card: storage, management, external). The A2 generation builds its parameter plane from one 200GE RoCE port per NPU. Faults on the three planes are different event classes and are never added.
  • Many more fabric devices than NPUs: CloudMatrix384 has 14 L1–L2 optical links and 1.17 UB switch chips per NPU; at 100k cards that is 1,400,000 links and 116,732 UB chips. The L2 tier is non-blocking with every port used, so one link or one L2 chip failing costs 1/16 of a sub-plane's bandwidth, not connectivity.
  • Fault domains: card (1) · node (8) · compute cabinet (32 / 64 / ≈ 88) · SuperPoD (384 / 8,192) · communication cabinet (common cause for ≈ 28 L2 chips) · cluster services (the whole job). The in-cabinet and communication-cabinet internals of Atlas 950 / 960 are not public; HAC-D's atlas950 preset reuses the CloudMatrix384 radix (numbers marked * in Table 2).

What this means for the allocation: the catalog states FIT per card-hour, and the architecture decides over how many devices those FIT spread and how many cards one event stops. With the CloudMatrix384 geometry, OPT 66.7 FIT per card ≈ 4.8 FIT per link, FABRIC 66.7 ≈ 57 FIT per UB chip, POWER 166.7 ≈ 5,333 FIT per compute cabinet (doubled for Atlas 950's 64-card cabinets). HAC-D's count scopes (l1_switch, l2_switch, subplane, optical_link, comm_rack, supernode) are exactly the input of WP1 of the v3.8 plan (architecture-derived AGREE weights).
